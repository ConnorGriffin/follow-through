# Four-day-workweek trial

## Expected

Maya predicted at least 25 percent more completed deep-work sessions with no
material decline in team response time or Thursday evening energy.

## Actual

Deep-work sessions increased by 4 percent. Team response time remained stable.
Thursday work ran past the planned stop time in four of six weeks, displacing
three shared evening plans. Fridays became useful recovery and errand days, but
did not create the expected focused capacity earlier in the week.

## Surprises

Removing Friday meetings reduced calendar fragmentation without increasing
finished design work. The administrative load moved into Thursday instead of
shrinking.

## Causes and evidence

Calendar records support the longer Thursday pattern. Session counts support
the small output change. It is plausible that compressing the same workload
caused the energy cost, but one six-week trial cannot separate schedule design
from a demanding project phase.

## Proposed learnings

- End this trial; its stated success threshold was not met.
- Treat “a compressed week improves Maya's focus” as unsupported, not disproved.
- Do not create a permanent preference against four-day schedules from this
  single event.
- A future experiment could reduce meeting load without compressing five days
  of work into four.
